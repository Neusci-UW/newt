Spines and plasticity: animations and calculators

Mockup for plasticity.html. Animations A–C are scripted loops (not the model) so they can be compared; the calculators run the page's model.

What to pick

  • Animations: A replaces the page's picture in all three parts. B and C are additions, one or both, for Part 3 only. My suggestion: A + C. A puts each step on the picture students already use. C is how growth is actually seen in experiments: the same spine imaged before and after. B is the most detailed, but it is a new, large drawing for one idea.
  • Calculators (every other lesson ends with one): 1 and 2 are high-yield; 3 is short and optional. They could be tabs of one calculator card, as on the Synapse page.

A · The page's picture, animated all parts

The same soma, dendrite and two spines. New: a spike runs down the axon, a vesicle fuses, glutamate crosses the cleft, the Mg²⁺ plug moves out of the NMDA pore, Ca²⁺ streams in through it and leaks down the neck, and labels show which enzyme is on. For LTP and LTD, AMPA receptors move in from a store inside the head (or back into it) one at a time, the head grows or shrinks, and a dashed outline keeps its starting size.

B · Close-up of the spine head Part 3, beside the charts

Zoomed in on one synapse: the presynaptic membrane, the cleft, the postsynaptic density (dark band), and the head below. LTP: Ca²⁺ switches on CaMKII (hexagons), which gather at the density, and vesicles carrying AMPA receptors fuse with the membrane (exocytosis). The density widens and the head swells. LTD: calcineurin (diamonds) switches on, and receptors are pulled into a coated pit and carried away (endocytosis).

C · Time-lapse of one spine Part 3, under the EPSP chart

Snapshots of spine A at four times, like two-photon imaging of a single spine (Matsuzaki et al. 2004). The head's size and AMPA count are given under each snapshot, with spine B unchanged as the control. In the page they would fill in as the hour plays, in step with the EPSP chart.

Calculator 1 · NMDA receptors and the Mg²⁺ block high-yield

The classic NMDA I–V curve: with Mg²⁺ the current shrinks toward zero at negative voltages, giving the J shape; without Mg²⁺ it is a straight line, like AMPA's. Ca²⁺ entry is drawn too: it peaks between −30 and −10 mV, where the receptors are unplugged but the driving force on Ca²⁺ is still large.

Calculator 2 · LTP or LTD? The Ca²⁺ rule high-yield

Dudek and Bear's experiment: the same 900 pulses at different frequencies. Low frequencies weaken the synapse (LTD), high ones strengthen it (LTP). Mockup note: in the page's model as it is, the switch comes between 50 and 100 Hz; in Dudek and Bear's slices it was near 10 Hz. Making Post's summation slower (τ 15 → about 50 ms) would bring it closer, at the cost of retuning Part 3. Set a protocol and see its Ca²⁺, the two enzymes, and the predicted change; the chart shows the whole frequency curve with your protocol on it.
