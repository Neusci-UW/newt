From one cell to the next: the chemical synapse

A spike arrives at the end of an axon. Ca²⁺ rushes in, vesicles release transmitter, it crosses the gap and opens channels on the next cell. Those channels are just another conductance with its own battery, so the next cell's voltage moves toward that battery.

How to startGoals
  • 1 · One spike Watch Ca²⁺ come in, a few vesicles fuse, transmitter cross the cleft and AMPA channels open: a small EPSP.
  • 2 · No Ca²⁺ outside The spike still arrives, but nothing is released.
  • 3 · Make it inhibitory Switch the receptor to GABAA: Cl⁻ channels, Erev near rest, an IPSP.
  • 4 · Hold at Erev Clamp the next cell at 0 mV: the channels still open, but no current flows.
  • 5 · NMDA and Mg²⁺ At rest NMDA barely passes current; depolarise and the Mg²⁺ block lifts.

Theory

Spike to releaseTransmitters and receptorsThe synaptic currentExcitatory or inhibitory?Ending the signalReferences
  • Spike to release: voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels at the active zone, Ca²⁺ triggers vesicle fusion, quanta (one vesicle = one mini). Steep Ca²⁺ dependence in a toggle.
  • Transmitters and receptors: glutamate → AMPA/NMDA, GABA → GABAA, glycine; ionotropic vs metabotropic in one paragraph.
  • The synaptic current: Ohm's law again: Isyn = gsyn(V − Erev). Erev of a mixed Na⁺/K⁺ channel from chord conductance (derivation in a toggle).
  • Excitatory or inhibitory? Decided by Erev vs threshold, not by the transmitter. Shunting inhibition; links forward to Summation.
  • Ending the signal: diffusion, uptake, acetylcholinesterase; NMDA Mg²⁺ block and coincidence detection.
