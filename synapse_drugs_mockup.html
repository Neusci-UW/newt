How to start: new chips

  • Botulinum toxin The spike arrives and Ca²⁺ comes in, but no vesicle fuses: flaccid paralysis.
  • Myasthenia gravis Antibodies have removed most nicotinic receptors: the EPSP is small and fails along a train of spikes.
  • …then neostigmine ACh lingers in the cleft, so the few receptors left open again and again: the EPSP grows back.
  • Curare ACh is released but can't bind: nothing opens.
  • Lambert–Eaton Antibodies against the Ca²⁺ channels: little release at first, more along a fast train as Ca²⁺ builds up.
  • A benzodiazepine With GABAA: the same GABA opens more channels, a bigger IPSP.

Drugs, toxins and diseases

Pre: terminal
Tetrodotoxin (TTX)blocks Na⁺ channels: the spike never reaches the terminal
Lambert–Eaton antibodiesremove most of the Ca²⁺ channels: less Ca²⁺ comes in
Botulinum toxincuts the proteins that fuse vesicles: no release
Cleft
Uptake blockernames itself for the transmitter: neostigmine (blocks acetylcholinesterase), TBOA (glutamate transporters), tiagabine (GABA transporter, an antiepileptic). Clearing is 10× slower.
Post: receptors
Competitive blockernames itself for the receptor: curare, CNQX, APV, bicuculline, strychnine (today's tick box, moved here)
Myasthenia gravisantibodies remove ~70% of the receptors
BenzodiazepineGABAA only: GABA binds more readily, more channels open
BarbiturateGABAA only: each channel stays open longer
KetamineNMDA only: plugs open channels, like Mg²⁺ but voltage won't push it out
